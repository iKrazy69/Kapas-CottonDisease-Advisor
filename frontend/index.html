    What Kapas can and cannot recognise

    Kapas is a research prototype from a university project. It suggests; it does not diagnose. Before spraying anything, check with your Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture officer.

    How Kapas decides what to say

    An advisor that always answers will sometimes be wrong. Kapas is built to hold back when the evidence is thin.

    1. 1

      Photo in

      A large photo is shrunk in your browser, then sent to the app running on this computer.

    2. 2

      Closest guesses

      A support vector machine, trained on labelled cotton leaf photos, scores six classes. Its top guess needs 70%, and that disease needs a fair record in testing, before Kapas calls it a match.

    3. 3

      Guide search

      Your question becomes a search through the CICR guide, held in a local vector database. Only passages that match go to the language model.

    4. 4

      Answer and sources

      The answer names the guide documents it came from. If nothing in the guide fits, Kapas says so instead of guessing.

    Three ways Kapas can answer

    A possible match

    The top guess clears the threshold. Kapas shows it, the test score for that disease, and what the guide says.

    Not sure

    The score is low, or the model has a weak record on that disease. Kapas says it is not sure and points you to your agriculture officer.

    Nothing found

    No passage in the guide fits the question. Kapas says that. Only if the internet lookup is on may it add a web answer, clearly labelled.

    The crop behind the cloth

    Cotton is the world’s leading natural fibre, grown in more than 70 countries. China, India, Brazil, the United States and Pakistan lead production.

    Pests, diseases and weather all pull yield down. That is why advice a farmer can trace to a source matters.

    Four species, all grown in India

    Gossypium hirsutum
    Upland cotton. Over 90% of the world’s cotton fibre.
    G. arboreum
    Desi, or Asian, cotton.
    G. herbaceum
    Levant cotton.
    G. barbadense
    Sea Island cotton.

    Bt cotton

    Bt cotton reached Indian fields as Bollgard in 2002 and Bollgard II in 2006. It now covers over 95% of the country’s cotton area, which has shifted which pests matter most.

    Source for this section: ICAR-CICR guide, introduction.
